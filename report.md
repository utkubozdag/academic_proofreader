# Proofreading Summary Report

- Conciseness: 2
- Style: 2

## Detailed Findings

### Clarity
1. **Hence,** -> *Specifically,*
   - The transitional word "Hence" implies a direct logical deduction, which is not strongly supported by the preceding sentence. "Specifically" or "In general" is more appropriate for elaborating on a finding that was just introduced with a caveat.

### Conciseness
1. **existing recent knowledge** -> *recent research*
   - This change removes redundancy for improved conciseness. "Recent research" or "the recent literature" inherently implies existing knowledge.
2. **further research is still needed** -> *further research is needed*
   - The adverbs "further" and "still" are redundant when used together in this context. Removing one improves conciseness without altering the meaning.

### Grammar
1. **content subject, matter,** -> *subject matter*
   - "Subject matter" is the correct compound noun. The original phrasing is redundant and grammatically awkward.

### Style
1. **The objective is to understand** -> *This review examines*
   - Replacing the nominalization ("The objective is to understand") with an active verb ("examines") creates a more direct and concise academic style.
2. **misinformation spread on the Internet.** -> *the spread of misinformation online.*
   - This revision improves readability and flow by using a more standard academic phrase. "Online" is also a more modern and concise term than "on the Internet."
